Random placement is clumpy — why sparse/clustered targets are generic

Drop M targets independently and uniformly into K = M cells (one per cell on average). You might expect a tidy, even field. You never get one. Instead cells sit empty while others stack two, three, more — the maximum-entropy state of a placed field is clumpy, and a perfectly even arrangement is exponentially rare (P ~ e−M). This is why the foraging mechanism, tuned to nothing, is matched to the world: resources are clumped by default.

Watch the right-hand plot fill in as you run trials — it is the distribution the paper's entropy figure plots, built by Monte Carlo. Mass piles up near the typical value e/M ≈ 1/e ≈ 0.37; the even end (e/M = 0) is never visited.
