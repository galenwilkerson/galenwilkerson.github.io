Cascade-mediated recruitment swarm — Lévy exploration + LTM recruitment on a torus

Inactive agents explore by Lévy flights; agents recruited by a linear-threshold cascade (fraction rule a/k ≥ θ) converge toward their recruiting parents — flowing toward the seed nodes on each resource. Blocking is emergent: crowding raises degree, drops a/k below θ, and releases agents back to exploration, balancing exploration and exploitation. Sliders push the swarm out of the cascade window.

The three presets are derived from a 4096-run global sweep over all 14 controls: each is the median of the top ~2% of actual joint settings (not the best value of each slider taken separately, which produces a frozen swarm that scores well by doing nothing), with a throughput floor so "efficient" cannot mean "inactive". The spread is roughly 1000× in steps per target consumed, dominated by the Lévy exponent α: wild superdiffusive jumps (α ≈ 0.55, Worst) make agents overshoot everything, while α ≈ 1.65 (Best movement) is far cheaper. Movement and communication optimise in genuinely different regimes, so there are two "best" buttons and the costs are never summed. Best movement wants near-Brownian search on a well-connected graph; Best comms wants a sparser graph (ρr≈1.4 vs 3.6) so cascades stay local — and pays with ~3× the movement. Note that bits/target is largely a clock: presence beacons fire once per agent per tick whatever the swarm does, so total bits is ~93% determined by how long the job takes. The recruitment-specific cost is the activation channel, which is density dependent and traces the cascade inverted-U — watch that tile to see what recruiting actually costs.
